# Where each house drink comes from, in time

The owner's boundary is on record in `docs/TODO.md`: **nothing that only exists after the 1980s, on the
non-alcoholic side.** This page is the pass that boundary was applied with — one row per drink we wrote ourselves,
each with the earliest thing I can point at and a grade saying how good that pointer is.

**Three grades, and they are not decoration:**

| Grade | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| `documented` | There is a dated event, patent or publication behind it |
| `tradition` | It is a long-standing practice, and the sources say "traditional" rather than giving a year |
| `assembled` | The elements are all old, the combination is ours, and nobody has called it a drink before us |

An `assembled` row is not a failure. It is what the boundary leaves room for: putting three old things in one glass
is not the same as inventing a drink in 2010. The row says so instead of dressing the combination up as a tradition.

## What this page is not

It is not a claim that every one of these is a "classic". **Six of the fifty-three are marked `assembled`** — the five
fizzes whose pairing nobody called a drink before us, and the peach iced tea — and they say so rather than being
dressed up as traditions.

**The IBA list is not on this page on purpose.** It is a transcription of somebody else's three category pages, the
owner said alcohol is not limited by era, and 35 of its 103 entries are the New Era drinks that fall after the line.
